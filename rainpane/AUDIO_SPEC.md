# Rainpane — Audio & Acoustics Specification

## 1. Purpose

Rainpane audio should sound like rain interacting with a pane of glass and the environment beyond it, not like a generic “rain sounds” loop.

The acoustic system is driven by the same simulated events/state used by the visual rain and water solver.

Primary design principle:

```text
one rain process
→ visual impact / water state
→ acoustic impact / runoff state
```

Do not run a separate fake audio-rain generator that has no causal relationship to the simulation.

---

## 2. Research basis

### 2.1 Water-drop impact acoustics

**“Sound generation by water drop impact on surfaces,” Experimental Thermal and Fluid Science (2020).**

Relevant findings for Rainpane:

- single-drop impacts produce transient acoustic energy over a broad audible band,
- spectrum/timbre depends on the impacted surface condition,
- dry solid, thin liquid film, and deeper liquid layers do not produce identical responses,
- the local water state therefore matters to impact sound.

Implementation consequence:

A drop impacting dry or nearly dry glass must not use the same synthesis/sample parameters as a drop striking an already-wet film/rivulet.

### 2.2 Impact/spreading on glass

**Roux & Cooper-White (2004), “Dynamics of water spreading on a glass surface.”**

Relevant principle:

- impact/spreading dynamics and acoustic response are linked to the same physical event.

Implementation consequence:

Use the same event payload for both visual impact dynamics and sound:

```text
diameter / mass
impact velocity
impact angle
impact location
local water thickness
local wetness
```

Do not derive sound from rendered sprite size after the fact.

### 2.3 Building/rainfall noise literature

**ISO 140-18 / ISO 10140 rainfall-noise measurement literature.**

Relevant principle:

Rain-generated noise depends on the statistics of impacts and the receiving structure. Drop distribution and velocity matter.

Implementation consequence:

`RainIntensity` must alter acoustic event density and spectral/timbral statistics, not only master gain.

### 2.4 Dripping-water warning

**Phillips, Agarwal & Jordan (2018), “The sound produced by a dripping tap is driven by resonant oscillations of an entrained air bubble,” Scientific Reports.**

Relevant finding:

The familiar tonal `plink` of a dripping tap is dominated by an entrained air bubble after a drop enters a liquid pool.

Implementation consequence:

Do not use generic faucet/dripping-water `plink` assets as the main sound of raindrops hitting glass. The physical mechanism is different.

## 3. Acoustic scene model

Rainpane should contain five principal sound classes.

### Layer A — exterior/environment rain

Purpose: communicate the larger rain field beyond the pane.

For the default forest scene, this may include:

- diffuse rainfall,
- rain striking leaves/ground at a distance,
- very subtle forest/air ambience.

This layer is broad, continuous, and not tied one-to-one to pane impacts.

It should provide space and continuity while the pane-impact layer supplies close detail.

### Layer B — dry-glass impacts

Short, close transients generated by drops striking relatively dry glass.

Expected qualities:

- short attack,
- crisp/broadband detail,
- low total duration,
- size/velocity-dependent weight,
- subtle variation by impact position.

Do not make all impacts identical pitched clicks.

### Layer C — wet-glass / thin-film impacts

Impacts into wet glass, a thin water film, or an existing rivulet.

Expected qualitative difference from dry glass:

- softer/less brittle attack,
- altered high-frequency content,
- more damped/wet transient quality,
- possibly broader/noisier texture for larger impacts.

Blend continuously according to local simulated water state rather than switching abruptly between two samples.

### Layer D — heavy runoff / sheet-water texture

This represents genuinely high water flux across the glass, not every visible rivulet.

It should be driven by metrics such as:

```text
totalFlux = integral(|q|)
activeRunoffArea
meanThickness over moving regions
highFluxArea
```

Expected behavior:

- near silent or absent in Drizzle,
- subtle in ordinary Rain,
- increasingly present in Heavy/Storm,
- reacts to actual solver state rather than only slider value.

A single thin slowly moving rivulet should normally be acoustically negligible.

### Layer E — boundary / drain / exiting-water events

When water leaves the simulated pane at the lower or side boundary, aggregate exiting mass can generate occasional events.

Possible cues:

- heavier wet ticks,
- short drainage splashes,
- subtle intermittent trickle texture under sustained high exit flux.

Do not trigger a loud sound for every grid cell crossing the boundary. Aggregate/cluster exit events.

## 4. Simulation-to-audio interface

`audio.js` should consume a narrow normalized event/state interface.

### 4.1 Impact event

Suggested payload:

```js
{
  type: 'impact',
  time,
  x, y,               // normalized pane position
  diameter,
  mass,
  normalVelocity,
  tangentialVelocity,
  impactEnergy,
  localThickness,
  localWetness,
  existingBody,       // boolean
  bodyThickness       // optional
}
```

### 4.2 Merge event

Only emit for merges that are large/energetic enough to matter acoustically.

```js
{
  type: 'merge',
  time,
  x, y,
  massA,
  massB,
  relativeVelocity,
  resultingMass
}
```

Most ordinary slow merges should produce no explicit sound.

### 4.3 Surface metrics frame/update

Do not send the full water grid into the audio engine each audio quantum. Expose reduced metrics:

```js
{
  wetFraction,
  meanThickness,
  movingWaterFraction,
  totalFlux,
  highFluxArea,
  runoffBodyCount,
  rainMassFlux,
  rainIntensity
}
```

Update at a moderate control rate such as 10–30 Hz.

### 4.4 Boundary-exit aggregate

```js
{
  type: 'exit',
  time,
  edge,
  mass,
  flux,
  xOrY
}
```

Prefer temporal aggregation rather than thousands of microscopic events.

## 5. Impact timbre mapping

Impact sound should depend on several variables, not merely `volume = radius`.

### 5.1 Energy

A useful perceptual driver:

```text
impactEnergyProxy ∝ mass * normalVelocity²
```

Use this to influence:

- gain,
- transient duration,
- amount of lower-frequency body,
- probability that the event remains individually salient in a dense cluster.

### 5.2 Size

Larger drops may tend toward:

- stronger low/mid-frequency content,
- slightly longer decay,
- larger total energy.

Small drops may favor:

- lighter, shorter, relatively higher-frequency taps.

Avoid deterministic pitch-by-size scales that sound musical/game-like.

### 5.3 Wetness

Use normalized local wetness/thickness to morph between dry and wet response.

Conceptually:

```text
wetMix = saturate(f(localThickness, localWetness))
```

Then vary:

- filter center/bandwidth,
- transient brightness,
- noise/resonance ratio,
- decay,
- sample family blend.

### 5.4 Position

Use x-position for subtle stereo pan.

Do not hard-pan edge impacts fully left/right. The listener is hearing a pane in front of them, not an arcade game.

Vertical position may very subtly affect gain/filtering if desired, but is not required.

## 6. Dense rain event handling

At Heavy/Storm intensity, the simulation may generate far more impacts than should become individual Web Audio voices.

### Required clustering

Bin impact events into small time windows, approximately 10–30 ms depending on tuning.

Within each bin:

- preserve a few largest/highest-energy events as discrete salient impacts,
- aggregate the many tiny impacts into one or a few procedural texture bursts,
- preserve approximate left/right energy distribution,
- preserve dry/wet mixture statistics.

This avoids:

- hundreds of simultaneous voices,
- machine-gun repetition,
- CPU spikes,
- acoustically impossible hyper-detail.

### Voice pooling

Use bounded pools for short transient sources/nodes where practical.

The audio engine must have an explicit maximum event-voice budget.

---

## 7. Preferred synthesis architecture

Use a **hybrid procedural + sample** design.

### Procedural components

Useful Web Audio building blocks:

- short shaped noise bursts,
- band-pass/high-pass/low-pass filtering,
- very short resonant components,
- transient gain envelopes,
- subtle stereo panning,
- offline-generated reusable noise buffers.

Procedural synthesis is especially useful for dense small impacts because parameters can vary continuously with physics state.

### Recorded sample components

Use a small curated set of real recordings if available:

- small drop on dry glass,
- medium/large drop on dry glass,
- drop on wet glass/thin film,
- heavier wet impact,
- drainage/edge splash.

Recorded samples should provide timbral realism, but never dictate event physics.

Randomize modestly:

- gain,
- playback rate within a narrow range,
- filtering,
- start offset if safe,
- stereo pan.

Avoid obvious repeated identical sample playback.

## 8. Exterior rain ambience

The continuous ambience bed should scale semantically with intensity.

Do not only increase gain.

Possible mappings:

### Drizzle

- sparse/delicate high-frequency detail,
- low broadband density,
- lots of acoustic space.

### Light / Rain

- fuller broadband bed,
- more leaf/ground texture,
- impact layer becomes more continuous statistically.

### Heavy / Storm

- denser low/mid body,
- stronger diffuse wash,
- possible wind layer,
- runoff layer becomes active because the solver actually contains more moving water.

Use crossfades, granular layering, or multiple long beds to avoid obvious loop boundaries.

---

## 9. Runoff audio mapping

Do not compute runoff volume directly from the user rain slider.

Instead derive a target runoff activity from solver metrics.

Example conceptual control:

```text
runoffActivity = f(
  totalFlux,
  highFluxArea,
  movingWaterFraction,
  meanMovingThickness,
  exitingMassRate
)
```

Smooth this value over time to avoid pumping.

Use it to control:

- gain of runoff texture,
- spectral density,
- number/frequency of boundary drainage events,
- optional trickle/granular activity.

If rain slider remains high but the pane is temporarily relatively dry/reset, runoff sound should not instantly remain at maximum.

---

## 10. Coalescence audio

Most merges are visually important but acoustically weak.

Rules:

- small slow bead-bead merge: usually silent,
- larger merge with meaningful relative velocity: optional subtle wet transient,
- rivulet joining: usually no explicit `pop`; sound consequences emerge through increased downstream flux,
- large impact into existing flow: handled primarily as a wet impact event.

Do not turn capillary coalescence into a bubble-popping game.

---

## 11. Pane structural resonance

Rainpane does not need a full finite-element model of a real glass panel for v0.1.

However, a very subtle common resonant/filter character can make impact events feel like they hit the same pane rather than arbitrary surfaces.

Possible approximation:

- one or several lightly resonant filtered components shared across impact synthesis,
- low Q / short decay,
- amplitude dependent on impact energy,
- avoid obvious pitched ringing.

The structural response must remain secondary to the impact transient.

---

## 12. Forest/default-scene spatial character

The built-in forest scene should sound as though the listener is indoors/behind a pane looking into a rainy forest.

Desired balance:

- close pane impacts = crisp and spatially local,
- exterior rain/foliage = diffuse and slightly distant,
- runoff = attached to the pane, centered/broad rather than far outdoors,
- optional room tone = extremely subtle.

Do not make the forest ambience so loud that it masks the glass detail.

---

## 13. Thunder and lightning — later

Thunder is not required for the first audio milestone.

If added:

- lightning visual event occurs first,
- thunder delay derives from virtual distance,
- avoid frequent/repetitive thunder,
- use multiple assets or procedural variation,
- thunder intensity must not be tied one-to-one to every Storm frame.

This layer is atmospheric, not part of glass-impact acoustics.

---

## 14. Browser lifecycle and UX

### Autoplay

Web Audio/audio playback may begin only after a user gesture.

### Mute

Provide a clear immediate mute toggle.

Mute must stop/silence:

- ambient bed,
- pending impact voices,
- runoff texture,
- edge events,
- optional thunder/wind.

### Hidden page

When document is hidden:

- suspend or substantially reduce event scheduling,
- pause/suspend ambience when appropriate,
- avoid accumulating a backlog of impact events to play on return.

### Resume

On resume, restart from current simulation state without replaying old impacts.

---

## 15. Performance targets

Primary target: current iPhone/iPad Safari.

Audio target:

- no audible dropouts under Storm,
- bounded voice count,
- bounded event queue,
- no per-impact heavy object allocation at extreme event rates,
- control-rate state updates rather than copying full physics fields.

Prefer stable audio over preserving every microscopic simulated impact as an audible event.

---

## 16. Suggested audio module architecture

```text
src/audio.js
  AudioEngine
    start()/resume()/suspend()
    setMuted()
    setMasterGain()
    setRainIntensityHint()
    handleImpact(event)
    handleMerge(event)
    handleExit(event)
    updateSurfaceMetrics(metrics)

  ImpactClusterer
  ImpactSynth
  AmbienceBed
  RunoffLayer
  BoundaryDrainLayer
  VoicePool
```

`RainIntensityHint` may help anticipate ambience changes but must not directly override wetness/flux-driven layers.

## 17. v0.1 acoustic acceptance tests

1. Sound does not begin before a valid user gesture.
2. Mute silences all Rainpane sound immediately.
3. Exterior ambience and close glass impacts are perceptually separate layers.
4. Dry-glass impacts differ audibly from impacts into wet film.
5. Larger/higher-energy impacts tend to sound stronger/heavier without becoming pitched game effects.
6. Impact x-position produces subtle stereo localization.
7. Audio impact timing is causally/statistically tied to simulated pane impacts.
8. Heavy rain does not create one audio voice per microscopic impact.
9. Storm remains free of obvious machine-gun sample repetition.
10. Event clustering preserves occasional large salient impacts.
11. Thin slow rivulets do not emit constant fake running-water loops.
12. Runoff sound increases when simulated surface flux increases.
13. Resetting/drying the pane reduces runoff sound even if ambient rain remains high.
14. Significant exiting-water flux can produce occasional boundary/drain events.
15. Ordinary small merges are usually silent.
16. Generic faucet/bubble `plink` is not used as the main glass-impact sound.
17. Hiding/resuming the page does not replay a backlog of old impacts.
18. Audio remains stable on current iPhone/iPad under Storm load.

---

## 18. Non-goals for first audio version

Not required:

- full structural modal/FEM simulation of a real pane,
- physically measured room impulse response,
- binaural head-related transfer functions,
- exact acoustic radiation model for every droplet geometry,
- microphone input,
- weather recording synchronization,
- physically exact thunder propagation.

The goal is causal, state-aware, perceptually convincing rain-on-glass audio that shares the same physics events as the visual simulation.
